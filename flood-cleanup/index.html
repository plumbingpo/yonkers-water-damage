Whether the water came from a storm, an overflowing river, a backed-up sewer, a failed sump pump or a burst plumbing line, flood damage needs a trained crew right away. Expect gray water (Category 2) or black water (Category 3) in almost any flood, and with it bacteria, contamination and potentially hazardous substances that threaten your health. Yonkers Restoration handles emergency flood cleanup across Yonkers and answers 24/7 to pump out the water, sanitize what it touched and restore your property safely.

The first job is getting the water out quickly, and our submersible and truck-mounted pumps are able to clear thousands of gallons every hour. Next our technicians, who hold IICRC certification, judge the level of contamination and choose a sanitizing plan that covers HEPA air filtration, antimicrobial application and disinfecting each surface that got wet. Anything unsalvageable comes out and is hauled off correctly, including wet insulation, drywall that took in contaminated water and waterlogged carpet padding.

Once the space is extracted and sanitized, our team brings in commercial drying gear, namely low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers plus high-velocity air movers, so the structural components dry all the way through. Daily readings from calibrated moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera tell us when everything is fully dry, which is what keeps mold from growing. Your insurance adjuster will want scope-of-work reports, equipment logs, moisture maps and photographs, so we keep that documentation going through the whole project.

Signs You Need Flood Cleanup in Yonkers

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water collecting along the foundation because the yard is saturated
  • Seepage at door thresholds, window wells or cracks in the foundation
  • Mud, silt or debris left on floors once the water goes down
  • Living areas flooded by contaminated water from a backed-up drain or sewer
  • Roof or wall openings left by a storm, with rain pouring through
  • Stains along baseboards and lower walls that mark the flood level
  • An overflowing washing machine, dishwasher or water heater

Why Flood Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

Getting the standing water out is only one part of cleaning up after a flood. Chemicals, bacteria, sewage and other contamination ride in with the flood and create real health dangers. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. Our technicians rely on psychrometric calculations, meter readings and thermal imaging to verify that each affected spot, from the framing to the subfloor to the inside of the walls, meets accepted drying standards. Nothing else reliably heads off secondary damage, and that damage can run thousands more to remediate, so we dry by the numbers.

How to Prevent Flood Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • A backwater valve on your sewer line keeps a backup from pushing sewage indoors
  • Elevate appliances and electrical systems in flood-prone parts of your property
  • Install a battery backup sump pump so you stay protected when storms knock out power
  • Being outside a designated flood zone does not rule out flooding, so think about flood coverage
  • Make sure the ground slopes away from your foundation on every side
  • Seal foundation cracks and apply a waterproofing coating to basement walls
