Toilet Overflow Cleanup Help in Yonkers

An overflowing toilet seems like something towels can handle, except the water keeps moving past the tile. The gap at the drain pipe, the wall and the space under the baseboard all take water in. Most important is what the water was carrying. If it came from the tank or the supply line, it is clean. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

In Yonkers, we begin each overflow job by determining the category of water, which decides how the rest is done. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. Black or gray water calls for protective equipment, taking out porous materials that got soaked, carpet pad for example, and disinfecting everything it contacted. Then we check the subfloor, the wall bases and the room underneath with moisture meters so nothing is left wet.

Before We Arrive

  1. Stop the WaterFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainHold off on flushing, and keep the washer, showers and sinks off too. If the drain line is blocked, any water you send down can come back up.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Check the Room BelowWith a second floor bathroom, inspect the ceiling of the room beneath. Should you see drips or a sag, cut the power to the room and get belongings out from under it.
  5. Block the SpreadLay down old towels so clean water does not travel into hallways or onto carpet. Leave contaminated water for a crew with proper equipment and disinfectants.
Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Tree roots or a collapsed section in the sewer line that slows every drain in the house.
  • Waste rising out of the lowest toilet in the house because the main sewer line is blocked.
  • A worn wax ring that lets water out at the base each time the toilet is flushed.
  • Blocked traps and drain lines, which leave the bowl nowhere to empty but onto the floor.
  • A child's toy, wipes or a wad of paper that gets flushed and sticks in the trap.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Swollen Baseboards and Trim

The bottom edge of a baseboard or door casing draws in water as soon as the floor floods. Since trim is usually fiberboard, it puffs up, cracks apart and stays deformed.

Bacteria in Building Materials

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Because disinfecting porous material is not reliable, it usually has to come out.

Ceiling Below

Water from an upstairs bathroom runs down the drain pipe and sits on the drywall ceiling underneath. Stains and sagging follow, and now and then the ceiling comes down.

Vanity and Drywall Wicking

The bottom of the drywall and the toe kick of the vanity take in water and pull it upward. The wall can be wet several inches above the floor while the paint still looks fine.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

When an overflow is accidental and sudden, like a clog nobody expected or a broken fill valve, homeowners policies often cover it. Water that backs up from the sewer or a drain is often excluded unless you added a backup endorsement, and that can apply to an overflow caused by a blocked main line. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • The type of bathroom flooring and whether it can be dried or must come up.
  • The amount of time that passed before the water was extracted and cleanup started.
  • Whether the toilet or vanity has to be pulled to dry and clean beneath it.
  • Category of water, as a contaminated overflow adds labor, protective measures and disinfecting.

What We Do

Our Toilet Overflow Cleanup Process

1

Identify the Category

Before any cleaning, our crew confirms whether this is supply water or water from the drain and bowl, and sets up containment and protective measures to match.

2

Extract and Remove

Our crew removes the standing water, takes out any baseboard, drywall or carpet pad that is contaminated, and pulls the toilet if water got below its base and the flange.

3

Clean and Disinfect

We clean the subfloor, fixtures, wall bases and floors, then treat them with antimicrobial products. When the water is Category 3, we finish this step before turning on any drying equipment.

4

Dry and Verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the bathroom and any affected room below. Our crew keeps metering the subfloor and the ceiling until both read normal again.
