Roof Leak Water Damage in Yonkers: What to Know

Where you see the stain and where the rain gets in are usually two different places. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. By the time a brown ring shows on the ceiling, the attic above it has often been wet for a while. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Contain the WaterSet buckets or pans under active drips and move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the way. Lay towels or plastic sheeting down to protect the floor.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingA ceiling that sags with trapped water can be drained: stay off to the side and make a small opening at its lowest spot, with a bucket underneath.
  4. Stay Off the RoofA wet roof and an unlit attic are both places to stay out of. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Photograph the DamageUse your phone to record the damaged ceiling, attic and belongings first, hold off on throwing things out until that is done, and call us to get the drying started.
Coverage

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. Our team gives your adjuster photos and moisture readings, yet what is covered comes down to your policy, so check it or talk to your agent.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Fasteners working loose over time, so water follows the nail shank into the attic.
  • Corroded or loose metal flashing where the roof meets a chimney, a vent or a wall.
  • Gutters packed with leaves, which push water back beneath the first row of shingles.
  • A fallen limb or large hail that punctures or bruises the roof covering.
  • Missing or broken shingles, often after high wind, with bare underlayment beneath.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Drywall Ceilings

Staining, sagging and loss of strength follow when drywall is not dried. Joints open, tape peels, and a waterlogged piece may fall without much notice.

Rafters and Decking

Rafters, trusses and decking that get wet again and again begin to darken, swell and soften. Decay here means less strength in what carries the roof.

Insulation in the Attic

For days after a leak, matted insulation sits wet on top of the ceiling and no longer insulates. Fiberglass can sometimes be dried. Cellulose that is saturated generally comes out.

Wall Cavities

Rain sometimes follows the framing into a wall, where the insulation and the hidden side of the drywall take it up. You might notice nothing more than a peeling patch or a puffy baseboard.
