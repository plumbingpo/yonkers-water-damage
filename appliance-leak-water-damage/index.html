Appliance Leak Water Damage in Yonkers: What to Know

Most of the appliance leaks we see trace back to three machines. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. Each one needs a different kind of cleanup.

Our Yonkers crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the Machine DownIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.
The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • A failed water inlet valve that keeps filling the washer or dishwasher after it should stop.
  • Refrigerators that leak at floor level because the drain pan has cracked or the defrost drain is clogged.
  • Dishwasher parts that wear out, including the door gasket, the drain hose and the seal on the pump.
  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • A washer drain hose that slips out of the standpipe, or a clogged standpipe.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Flooring and Subfloor

Hardwood, vinyl plank and laminate all let water slip underneath. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.

Wall Bases and Trim

Water standing on a laundry or kitchen floor wicks up into baseboards and the bottom of the drywall. Often the worst of it is on the wall in back of the appliance, where it went unseen.

Adjoining Rooms

A washer hose can release water for hours. It passes under walls into hallways, closets and bedrooms, and soaks carpet and pad well away from the laundry room.

Hidden Mold at the Back

The warm, dark space behind a refrigerator or dishwasher rarely gets checked. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.
