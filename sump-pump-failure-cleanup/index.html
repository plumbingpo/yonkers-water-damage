Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Yonkers

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Keep Out of the BasementDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceSee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallTake pictures of the pump and the water level, bring valuables that are still dry upstairs, and phone us. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
What We Do

How We Handle Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

1

Get the Water Out

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. Low spots, the area by the pit and the rest of the floor are cleared of water and silt.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Clean and Sanitize

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. This happens before drying so contaminants are not blown around the basement.

4

Dry and Confirm

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. Water backing up or overflowing from a sump is usually excluded under a standard homeowners policy, and a flood policy is a different product with rules of its own. Often the only coverage comes from an added endorsement for water backup and sump overflow, which has limits of its own. Check the declarations page for it, or call your agent to find out. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
  • More water flowing into the pit than an undersized pump can move.
  • A discharge line that is blocked, crushed or disconnected outside the house.
  • The pump being unplugged by accident or sitting on an outlet that tripped.
  • The float getting caught on the side of the basin or tangled in its cord.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Wall Framing

Bottom plates rest directly on concrete, so they are the first framing to get wet. We clean, disinfect and dry the studs and plates after removing the wet drywall.

Carpet and Flooring

Groundwater-soaked carpet and padding come out, as cleaning cannot make them safe. Vinyl plank and laminate floors generally have to be pulled as well, since dirty water stays under them.

Furnace and Water Heater

Mechanical equipment often shares the floor with the sump pit. If wiring, burners or control boards got wet they may be ruined, and no appliance should be restarted until it has been checked.

Outlets and Wiring

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.
