Leaking Pipe Water Damage Help in Yonkers

Most pipe leaks start without any warning. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. For Yonkers homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. That is our part of the job. Our crew maps the damp materials with thermal imaging and moisture meters, opens up no more than necessary, and runs dehumidifiers and air movers until the readings return to normal. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off the WaterClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Read the MeterShut off all taps and fixtures, then go look at the water meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographBefore a repair is made or anything goes in the trash, remove what is stored near the leak and get pictures of the pipe, the staining and the warping.
  5. Have the Area MeteredBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.
Coverage

Insurance and Leaking Pipe Water Damage

Insurers draw a line between a sudden leak and a slow one. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Check your policy and ask your agent. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Years of excessive water pressure straining valves and joints until one of them starts to weep.
  • Pinhole corrosion in older copper lines that slowly eats through the pipe wall.
  • Leaks at a sink drain joint or trap, which release a little water with every use.
  • A compression fitting or supply line connection at a sink or toilet that has loosened or worn with use.
  • Cold snaps that freeze piping in unheated spaces, leaving small cracks that leak after the thaw.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Swollen Cabinets

Particleboard cabinet bases under a sink swell and crumble once they absorb water. Because the board never shrinks back, a long leak often ends with a new cabinet base.

Decayed Wall Framing

After weeks of dampness, bottom plates and wall studs start to go soft and rot. Any piece too weak to carry load gets cut out and swapped for new lumber.

Wet Insulation

Insulation in the wall soaks up leak water like a sponge and holds it against the framing. It dries very slowly in a closed cavity, so saturated insulation is usually pulled out.

Stained Drywall and Ceilings

Leaks over a ceiling or behind a wall surface show as bubbling paint and brown stains. If the board keeps taking on water it loses its stiffness, starts to sag and may have to be removed.
