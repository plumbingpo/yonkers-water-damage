Why Sewage Backups Happen in Yonkers, NY

Home / Blog / Why Sewage Backups Happen in Yonkers, NY

Few household emergencies are as unpleasant as sewage backing up into the home. The result is extensive damage, an immediate threat to health, and a professional cleanup bill that can reach thousands of dollars whenever raw sewage enters the rooms you live in. Learn the reasons these backups happen, act on a few preventive measures, and you cut the odds considerably. This guide from Yonkers Restoration covers the common causes of sewage backups in Yonkers, NY and practical steps you can take to protect your property.

Why Sewage Backups Happen

When something keeps wastewater from draining to the city sewer or your septic tank as designed, it comes back into the building. Usual culprits are tree roots growing into sewer pipes; lines clogged by a buildup of hair, grease, and debris; pipes that are old or have collapsed; combined sewer overflow events; an overloaded municipal system in heavy rain; and toilets used to flush the wrong things, such as feminine products, wipes, and too much paper.

Tree Root Intrusion

Tree roots are one of the most common causes of sewer line blockages in Yonkers. Because roots grow toward water, a pipe that always carries it draws them in. They work their way in at cracks, joints, and connection points, then slowly fill the inside of the pipe until it blocks and backs up. Older sewer lines made of clay tile are especially easy for roots to invade. Having your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years can identify root problems before they cause a backup.

How to Prevent a Sewage Backup

Several habits and upgrades help keep sewage out of a Yonkers home, and the first is a backwater valve on the sewer connection, which stops backflow from the municipal system. Keep cooking oil and grease out of every drain. Do not flush feminine products, wipes, or anything other than toilet paper. Have the sewer line inspected and cleaned on a regular schedule. Deal with a slow drain quickly, before it turns into a full blockage. In an emergency someone will need the sewer cleanout, so learn where yours sits. It may also be worth looking at sewer line coverage or a backup endorsement for your homeowner's insurance.

Sewage Came Up? Do This First

Cleaning up a sewage backup on your own is something you should never try. It carries viruses, bacteria, parasites, and other dangerous pathogens capable of making you seriously ill.
