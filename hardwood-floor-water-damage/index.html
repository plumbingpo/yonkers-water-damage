Hardwood Floor Water Damage in Yonkers

Hardwood responds fast when it gets wet. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. A floor in this condition is not necessarily ruined. When drying starts within the first few days, solid hardwood frequently lies flat again. What matters is removing moisture from the wood and from its subfloor steadily and under control.

Our tool for hardwood is the floor drying mat. Each mat is sealed against the floor and hooked to a vacuum unit, which pulls moisture upward through seams and boards as dehumidifiers dry the air in the room. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. No sanding should happen before the wood has dried completely. A cupped floor sanded early ends up crowned afterward.

Signs to Look For

  • Crowning, meaning the middle of each board bulges above its edges
  • Boards that feel soft or spongy underfoot or flex more than before
  • Cupping, where each board's edges rise above its middle
  • Sections of flooring that have risen, forming a tent shape along a seam
  • Nail heads and seams ringed by dark staining
  • Earthy odor at floor level even though the surface looks clean
The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Water slips through the seams and spreads between the boards and subfloor. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

24 to 48 hours

Edges Begin to Rise

The edges come up as each board expands. A cloudy look can appear in the finish. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Day 3 to 7

Pressure Builds Up

Expanding boards push harder and harder on one another. With fasteners losing their hold and boards compressing at the edges, some areas can lift away from the subfloor.

Past one week

Beyond Drying

Expect a good chance of mold below, dark staining and boards that have buckled. Drying is usually no longer enough, and the bulk of the wet flooring has to be replaced.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Stains From Nails and Staples

Staples and flooring nails corrode when moisture lingers, and their grip weakens. The rust also bleeds into the wood as dark stains that sanding may not remove.

Wet Subfloor

The subfloor can hold moisture long after the boards feel dry. Unless that layer is dried as well, expect renewed cupping or mold on the underside.

Growth Beneath the Floor

The space between hardwood and subfloor is dark and has no airflow. You will smell it before you see it, because nothing shows on the surface.

Drying Too Fast

When the top of the wood dries much quicker than the inside, stress builds in the board. The result can be cracks, splits and permanent gaps between boards.

What We Do

How We Handle Hardwood Floor Water Damage

1

Take Baseline Readings

Readings are taken in the boards and the subfloor, and on dry flooring close by as a reference. The comparison shows how far down moisture reached and how likely the floor is to recover.

2

Extract Standing Water

We extract any standing water immediately and clear furniture and rugs from the wet section. Boards swell less when the seams stop taking in water sooner.

3

Set Drying Mats

A vacuum system hooked to mats sealed on the surface lifts moisture out of the boards from above. The room is managed with dehumidifiers so drying stays even and the boards do not crack.

4

Track Progress and Report

We track readings every day or two until boards and subfloor match dry reference areas. Then we tell you whether the floor needs refinishing, a few boards replaced, or nothing.
