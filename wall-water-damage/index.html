Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in Yonkers

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

Warning Signs

  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • A tide line or discoloration running along the bottom of the wall
  • Swollen or warped baseboards with a gap opening behind them
  • One wall noticeably cooler under your hand than the rest
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • Wet tack strip, lifting planks or stained flooring at the base of the wall
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

Every wall is checked with a thermal camera and moisture meter, and we mark the height and reach of the water, covering the other face and the rooms next door.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Starts Climbing

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Days 3 through 7

Drywall Breaks Down

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Once wet, fiberglass insulation stays wet and keeps the studs damp. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Outlets and wiring are mounted near the floor, exactly where the water gathers. Do not use outlets on a wet wall until the circuit has been checked.

Mold Inside Cavities

Mold thrives on the rear face of drywall because it is paper covered, dark and has no moving air. That side may be coated in growth even as the room side appears spotless.

Water Next Door

It slips beneath the bottom plate and reaches the closet or room on the other side. We check both sides of every affected wall and the flooring along it.

Repair or Replace

What Stays and What Goes

We save what can be dried properly and remove only what cannot. Moisture readings make that call, not guesswork.
