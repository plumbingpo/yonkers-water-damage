Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. Do not confuse it with rebuilding. Mitigation covers the steps taken ahead of repairs so undamaged materials stay that way. In Yonkers, our team handles mitigation by controlling the source, removing the water, containing the wet area and stabilizing the structure. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

The words mitigation and restoration get used as if they were interchangeable. Each is its own phase of the job. The emergency stage is mitigation, where we stop the loss, keep it from spreading and stabilize the house. Restoration is what comes after: drying is confirmed, and damaged drywall, flooring and finishes are repaired or rebuilt. Because an insurer lists each one separately as well, knowing where you stand in the process is helpful.

A typical mitigation visit covers four things. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Second is extraction, to clear out standing water. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Stabilization, which means removing materials that cannot be saved, treating surfaces with antimicrobial, and setting air movers and dehumidifiers. Along the way, your belongings and furniture get moved or set up on blocks to keep them safe.

When to Call for Water Damage Mitigation in Yonkers

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water is no longer flowing, though it has left wet floors, cabinets or walls behind.
  • Wet materials have sat for a day and a musty smell is starting.
  • The space under your cabinets in the bathroom or kitchen took on water you cannot get to.
  • Along the floor, water is travelling out of one room and into another.
  • The insurance company instructed you to take reasonable action against further damage.
  • Following a water loss, indoor air stays damp and condensation forms on the glass.
  • After a leak, the baseboards have swollen or started separating from the wall.

How We Handle Water Damage Mitigation

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Control the Source

We shut off the supply, cap the failed line, or tarp and board the opening where water is entering. If a plumber or roofer is needed for the permanent fix, we coordinate the timing.

2

Remove Standing Water

We extract standing water using pumps and extraction units, then check carpet and its padding. Fast removal of bulk water limits damage more than drying equipment could by itself.

3

Contain Wet Areas

Wet rooms are closed off from dry ones with barriers of plastic sheeting and controlled airflow. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize the Structure

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. Moisture readings are recorded on every visit until the structure can be repaired, typically in 3 to 5 days.

The Benefits of Professional Water Damage Mitigation

Fast decisions, one after another, are what mitigation consists of. Can this section of drywall dry in place, or does it need cutting out? Which category applies, 1, 2 or 3, and did that change after the leak first began? Should the toe kick under the cabinets be pulled? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

Good mitigation also protects your claim. Most policies require the owner to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and insurers want proof it was done. On arrival we take photos, then log moisture readings every day and itemize whatever is removed. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. It also gives the repair contractor a clear starting point, with no wet surprises behind new drywall.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Mitigation

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Trade rubber washer hoses for braided steel, and renew them roughly every five years.
  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.
  • Install an automatic shutoff valve that closes the main when a leak is detected.
  • Seal gaps around windows, doors and roof penetrations before the next heavy rain arrives.
  • Regrade low spots beside the house where water gathers after rain.
  • Keep gutters clear and downspouts pointed away from the foundation of the house.
