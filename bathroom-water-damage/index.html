Bathroom Water Damage Help in Yonkers

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. In Yonkers, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

What It Looks Like

  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub
  • The bottom panel inside the vanity has swelled, stained or bowed
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
  • Slight rocking when you sit on the toilet, or a dark ring on the floor around it
  • Tiles near the tub that have loosened, shift when stepped on, or sound hollow
Our Process

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Pinpoint the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first few days

Water Gets Past

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold Behind Tile

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

The Room Below

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Light fixtures and wiring can be in its path.

A Failed Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. Up top everything looks fine, yet the framing underneath never dries.

Rot Under the Toilet

Each flush leaks a small amount once the wax ring fails. The water is category 3, and it rots the subfloor around the flange until the toilet starts to rock.
