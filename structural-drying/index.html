No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

Yonkers Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

Warning Signs for Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Warped casing or baseboard where water reached the bottom of the wall
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Stained, swollen or soft spots in the drywall
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak
  • Materials that feel damp or cool to the touch even after visible water is gone

Mapping the Moisture

Using moisture meters, thermal imaging and hygrometers, we create a comprehensive moisture map of all affected areas. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline readings.

2

Setting the Equipment

Commercial LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are strategically positioned based on our moisture map. The materials being dried determine how we set the airflow and the temperature.

Each day a technician visits to document progress, reposition equipment and measure moisture at every monitoring point. Along the way, we tell you where the drying schedule stands.

When moisture readings reach acceptable levels throughout all affected materials, we document final readings, remove equipment and certify that your property has reached dry standard.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Each unit is positioned according to the moisture map, so ask before shifting anything
  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Deal with water intrusion right away, since an early start on drying gives a better result
  • Make sure the technician can get in each day for monitoring visits

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.
