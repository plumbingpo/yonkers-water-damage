Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Yonkers

The tank on a water heater stores 40 to 80 gallons, yet a leak can release far more than that. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

In Yonkers, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Cut the Power or GasIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
  • Drain valves that crack, work loose or stop sealing completely.
  • Flex connectors or inlet and outlet fittings at the top of the heater that have corroded.
  • The tank rusting from within after its anode rod has worn away.
  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Adjacent Room Flooring

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Stored Belongings

Whatever is kept by a closet or garage heater, from keepsakes to furniture and boxes, is the first thing to get wet. Cardboard falls apart, and wet contents can mold if they are not dried or discarded quickly.

Platform and Subfloor

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. Rot can weaken it, a real concern once a full new tank sits on top.

Heater Closet Drywall

When the heater closet is indoors, its drywall draws water upward and passes it to rooms on the far side. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Our Process

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Extract the Water

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

3

Remove Wet Material

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. Attic jobs include removing wet insulation to expose the joists and the ceiling drywall for drying.

4

Dry and Monitor

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for the 3 to 5 days drying usually takes. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • How much insulation, ceiling or wall drywall needs to be taken out.
  • How many stored belongings got wet and need to be moved, dried or hauled away.
  • Any rebuilding of the subfloor or platform beneath the tank ahead of the new heater.
  • Placement of the unit, as leaks from an attic or an inside closet run higher than those in a garage.
