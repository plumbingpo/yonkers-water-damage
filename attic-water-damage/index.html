Attic Water Damage Help in Yonkers

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. In both cases, almost nobody climbs up to check. Our team in Yonkers inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

Signs to Look For

  • After rain, a stain or brown ring on a top floor ceiling that looks worse
  • Water droplets or rust on the tips of nails that poke through the roof sheathing
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • Attic ducts and metal hangers showing drips, rust or water marks
  • Spots of daylight at a chimney, a vent pipe, or a valley in the roof
  • Insulation that looks matted, flattened, or darker than the insulation around it
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

A Falling Ceiling

Saturated insulation is heavy. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.

Mold on Sheathing

The underside of the roof deck is a common place for mold when ventilation is poor. Before anyone looks, it may already have spread over a large section.

Leaks From Attic Equipment

In a lot of homes the attic houses an air handler along with its condensate line and pan. For weeks an overflowing pan or blocked drain can drip into insulation, and there is no storm to tip you off.

Lost Insulation Value

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Can Start

Damp wood and paper facing in a warm, still attic are ideal for mold. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

Weeks later

Ceiling Stains Appear

Repeated wetting saturates the insulation, and water gets down to the ceiling. Brown rings appear in the room below, and the drywall may start to sag.

Months later

Rot Sets In

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Trace

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. Its full reach is mapped with a thermal camera and meter readings.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. The access path is kept contained and the living area underneath is protected.

3

Dry the Wood

Rafters, sheathing and joists are dried by air movers and dehumidifiers until their moisture levels are normal. An antimicrobial goes on after any visible mold has been cleaned from the wood.

4

Fix the Cause

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. New insulation is installed to bring back the R-value after the attic is staying dry.
