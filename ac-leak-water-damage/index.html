AC Leak Water Damage in Yonkers: What to Know

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ACSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Bring In Two ProsAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • Bare refrigerant lines or ducts that sweat in humid weather and drip on the ceiling.
  • Failure of the small pump that lifts condensate to a drain.
  • More meltwater than the pan can handle after an iced-over coil thaws.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Mold Around the Air Handler

Mold may appear in 24 to 48 hours on damp materials by the air handler, where the return air stream can carry its spores through the ductwork to other rooms.

Floors Outside the Closet

Carpet pad, laminate and hardwood near the closet all get wet from underneath as the leak spreads past the door. Laminate swells and will not recover, and hardwood cups if it stays wet.

The Platform and Closet Walls

Many closet installations put the air handler on a wood platform, with the return box below it. Leaks decay the stand and climb the closet drywall, concealed by the equipment itself.

Trim and Baseboards

Trim at floor level, including the closet door frame, wicks up the leak. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

How We Handle AC Leak Water Damage

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • The number of days water kept dripping before the AC was turned off.
  • If mold is present, the added work of containing and removing it.
  • Square footage of ceiling, wall and flooring that has to be replaced.
  • The location of the air handler, since attic, garage and closet leaks spread differently.
